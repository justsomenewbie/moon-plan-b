# Sunlight at the start of a saved week

The comparison globe offers two dated lighting samples for the saved Odysseus and Athena comparisons. Each sample is the apparent subsolar point on the Moon at the specified UTC as seen from Earth's center. The selected instant remains fixed when the user rotates the globe. A single point describes the direction of incoming sunlight across the displayed sphere. The numbered site markers and the saved visibility totals have separate sources.

| UTC | Longitude, east | Latitude, north |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2026-11-14 00:00 | 126.617658° | −1.557279° |
| 2026-11-21 00:00 | 41.450414° | −1.541521° |

NASA/JPL Horizons observer quantity 15 returned `SunSub-LON` and `SunSub-LAT` for target Moon (301), observer Earth geocenter (500@399), and ephemeris DE441. The response specifies east-positive `MOON_ME` coordinates and a spherical reference radius of 1,737.4 km. Horizons includes Sun-to-Moon and Moon-to-observer light travel time. The archived rows and the SHA-256 of the retrieved response are in [the data file](../frontend/lib/horizons-weekly-sunlight.json). The retrieval banner can change on a repeated request. The separate `tableSha256` covers the decoded text after `$$SOE\n` and before `$$EOE`, including its trailing newline. Compare these UTC rows when reproducing the values. NASA Goddard's 2026 hourly catalog agrees with both coordinates to its published 0.001° precision. As a second check, the team's archived run B Sun-center azimuth and elevation from Odysseus and Athena were transformed from the two local east/north/up bases to lunar fixed coordinates. All four apparent directions agree with Horizons to within 0.00070°. These site directions provide a check on sign and orientation. NAIF documents the close alignment of the DE440 mean-Earth realization used by the team with the DE421 cartographic frame. [NASA Goddard hourly data](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a005500/a005587/mooninfo_2026.json) · [NAIF lunar frames kernel](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/fk/satellites/moon_de440_250416.tf)

The daylight intensity follows the positive part of the dot product between the spherical surface normal and the Sun direction. A display brightness floor of 13% preserves surface detail on the night side. This visualization omits local terrain shadows, penumbra, and eclipse obscuration. The map brightness has no units. It supplies no power, temperature, radio-link or site-visibility estimates. The source remains the reference map when no saved lighting sample is selected.
